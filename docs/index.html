Method

Flowers. Each of Berlin's 1,675 biotope types gets a forage score (0–1) and flowering months; urban types (courtyards, allotments, railway land, streets) included. Street and park trees add flowers by genus and month (bee-forage lists).

Islands. Cells with rich flowers (score ≥ 0.5 that month) whose gap is 100 m or less form one network: a typical hop for small solitary bees. Each 30 m hexagon takes the island it mostly lies on.

Highways. The 10 m grid is a network of resistors: sealed ground (Berlin sealing map 2021), tall buildings (OSM) and flowerless cells slow a bee down. 3,600 flower pairs are connected one at a time and the current is summed per cell (circuit theory, as in Circuitscape), weighted by month and range (300 m small bees, 1 km bumblebees). The strongest 8% of current, skeletonised, are the highways.

Additions. For each kind (628 bus and tram stops, 6,879 street-tree pits, 157 school and nursery yards, 768 car parks), a 30 m × 30 m flower bed joins every island within 100 m of it. The 25 places that raise the equivalent connected area most over April–September are picked one by one.

Limits

A model of potential, not measured nectar or verified flight routes. Scores are expert-style rules and need checking on the ground; wild bees differ widely by species.

Records cluster where people walk; no record does not mean no bees. 62% of records lie where the biotope map shows few flowers: the map misses balconies, front gardens and tree pits.
